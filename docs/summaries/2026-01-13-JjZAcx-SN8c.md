# 「攻城塔」之亂：Red Bull Wololo 聯賽的爭議性禁令完整解析

## 重點摘要
在 Red Bull Wololo 資格賽中，官方臨時宣布禁用「攻城塔」。此決定的主因是玩家濫用攻城塔作為高速、耐打的步兵「計程車」進行無法反制的攻擊，這尤其利用了「火長矛兵」的一個攻擊延遲 Bug。此禁令雖引發程序爭議，但獲得了絕大多數頂尖職業選手的支持，旨在挽救遊戲平衡與觀賽體驗。

## 故事大綱
- **開場：** 影片主講人（一名選手代表）介紹了 Red Bull Wololo 資格賽期間，「攻城塔」（Siege Tower）被官方禁用的重大爭議，並承諾將全面探討事件的始末與各方觀點。
- **中段：** 爭議的核心是攻城塔被玩家以非預期的方式濫用。它不再是用於攻牆，而是變成一台高速、高血量的「法拉利」，載著火長矛兵等單位快速突襲對手後方。這套戰術之所以無解，關鍵在於一個 Bug：火長矛兵離開塔後能立即發動攻擊，完全繞過了遊戲的平衡機制。主講人分析了禁令的優缺點，並反駁了「既然大家都能用，那就是公平的」論點，指出這將導致比賽極度單調（僅剩三四個文明可玩），淪為重複的換家戰術，嚴重破壞觀賽體驗。
- **結尾：** 主講人揭露，此禁令是基於選手議會中高達 80-90% 的選手投票共識，最終由官方採納並執行的。他澄清自己僅是收集意見的代表，並駁斥了那些指控他為個人利益操縱規則的「噁心」言論，同時以自己在使用攻城塔的環境下仍排名第一來證明自己能在任何環境下成功。他總結道，未來應在賽事開始前就預先解決這類潛在的濫用問題。

## 關鍵見解
1.  **濫用 vs. 設計原意：** 禁令的核心並非針對攻城塔的正常用途（跨越城牆），而是針對其作為高速運兵車的「法拉利」濫用，這完全破壞了遊戲「步兵-弓兵-騎兵」的相剋平衡。
2.  **火長矛兵 Bug 是催化劑：** 壓垮駱駝的最後一根稻草是火長矛兵的 Bug。它允許單位「出塔瞬間攻擊、立即回塔」，這種打帶跑戰術在技術純熟的選手手中是完全無解的，這已非「策略」而是「漏洞利用」。
3.  **觀賽體驗至上：** 如果放任此戰術，比賽將只剩下少數幾個文明的鏡像對決，且遊戲內容會變成單調的換家競速。這對作為電競生命線的觀眾而言，將是一場災難。
4.  **職業圈的共識：** 此決定並非由官方獨斷，而是來自選手議會的壓倒性投票結果。這反映了身處第一線的職業選手普遍認為該戰術已嚴重影響競技公平性。
5.  **程序與時機的兩難：** 儘管禁令有其必要性，但在資格賽中途更改規則仍引發了「不專業」的批評。這件事凸顯了未來大型賽事應在賽前更周全地審查規則與潛在漏洞的重要性。

## 精彩時刻
- **法拉利計程車：** 將攻城塔形容為運送步兵的「法拉利」，生動地描繪了其被濫用時的驚人速度與衝擊力。
- **"Can't beat it. Join it." (打不贏就加入)：** 頂尖選手 MBL 在對話中的無奈之言，精準表達了選手們在面對此無解戰術時，除了被迫跟進外別無選擇的困境。
- **君子協定：** 在官方介入前，選手們曾考慮達成「君子協定」來自發禁用攻城塔，這類似於在地圖生成不公時（如開局少樹）刪除村民的慣例，展現了選手社群的自律性。
- **數字不會說謊：** 影片結尾，主講人面對「為私利操縱規則」的指控，他直接展示自己在使用攻城塔的環境下仍是天梯第一名的事實，強勢回應：「數字不會說謊」。

# The Siege Tower Situation: A Full Breakdown of the Controversial Tournament Ban

## TL;DR
The Red Bull Wololo tournament admin banned the siege tower mid-qualifier due to a game-breaking exploit where it was used as an un-counterable, high-speed infantry "taxi," primarily abusing a bug with Fire Lancers. Despite controversy over its timing, the decision was backed by a vast majority of pro players to restore game balance and preserve the viewing experience.

## Story Flow
- **Beginning:** The speaker, a player representative, introduces the major controversy surrounding the mid-tournament ban of the Siege Tower in a Red Bull Wololo qualifier, promising a comprehensive look at all perspectives.
- **Middle:** The core issue is revealed: the siege tower was being abused in an unintended way. Instead of scaling walls, it became a high-speed, durable "Ferrari" for transporting infantry like Fire Lancers to execute devastating raids. This tactic was unstoppable due to a critical bug allowing Fire Lancers to attack instantly upon exiting the tower, bypassing normal balance mechanics. The speaker analyzes the pros and cons of the ban and refutes the "if everyone can do it, it's fair" argument, pointing out it would lead to a stale meta (only 3-4 viable civilizations) and a terrible viewing experience filled with repetitive base-trades.
- **End:** The speaker discloses that the ban was enacted by the admins after an overwhelming 80-90% vote in favor of it from the top players' council. He clarifies he was merely a representative gathering votes and refutes "disgusting" accusations that he manipulated the rules for personal gain, pointing to his #1 rank on the ladder while the siege tower was legal as proof of his ability to succeed in any meta. He concludes that such potential exploits should be addressed before tournaments begin in the future.

## Key Insights
1.  **Exploit vs. Intended Use:** The ban wasn't about the siege tower's normal function but its abuse as a high-speed "Ferrari" for troops, which completely broke the game's rock-paper-scissors balance between infantry, archers, and cavalry.
2.  **The Fire Lancer Bug was the Catalyst:** The final straw was the bugged interaction with Fire Lancers. It allowed for an "eject-shoot-retreat" tactic that was completely un-counterable in the hands of skilled players, making it more of a bug exploit than a strategy.
3.  **The Viewing Experience is Paramount:** Allowing this tactic to dominate would have reduced the tournament to mirror matches between a few civilizations and monotonous base-race scenarios. This would be a disaster for viewers, who are the lifeblood of the esports ecosystem.
4.  **Consensus Among the Pro Scene:** The decision was not made in a vacuum; it was driven by an overwhelming vote in the players' council. This shows that the players on the front lines widely agreed that the tactic was detrimental to competitive integrity.
5.  **The Dilemma of Procedure vs. Necessity:** While necessary, changing a rule mid-qualifier drew criticism for being unprofessional. The incident highlights the need for more thorough vetting of rules and potential exploits before major tournaments.

## Notable Moments
- **The Ferrari Taxi:** Describing the siege tower as a "Ferrari" vividly captures the incredible speed and impact it had when used as a troop transport exploit.
- **"Can't beat it. Join it.":** Top player MBL's quote in a chat, perfectly summarizing the helplessness players felt and why they were forced to adopt the broken strategy to stay competitive.
- **The Gentleman's Agreement:** The players had considered a "gentleman's agreement" to self-ban the tower, similar to their convention of deleting villagers for unfair map spawns, showing the community's proactivity before the official ruling.
- **"The numbers don't lie.":** At the end of the video, the speaker counters accusations of manipulating the rules for personal gain by showing he was #1 on the ladder even with the exploit being legal, making a powerful statement that his skill isn't meta-dependent.
